Foundational Duality & Benchmark Setup

Six-Node Benchmark Network & Max-Flow Min-Cut Duality

Unified capacitated directed network (|V| = 6, |E| = 9, Optimal Flow |f*| = 19) used across all four simulators

Canonical 6-Vertex, 9-Arc Directed Network Optimal |f*| = 19

1. Capacity & Flow Conservation Invariants

For a directed network G = (V, E) with source S and sink T:

0 ≤ f(u, v) ≤ c(u, v) ∀ (u, v) ∈ E
∑ f(u, v) = ∑ f(v, w) ∀ v ∈ V \ {S, T}

2. Residual Capacity & Reverse Flow

Residual network G_f tracks spare forward room and backward cancellation capacity:

c_f(u, v) = c(u, v) − f(u, v) + f(v, u)

3. Max-Flow Min-Cut Theorem (Ford & Fulkerson, 1956)

The maximum s–t flow value equals the minimum capacity over all disconnecting cuts (X, V \ X):

max |f| = min c(X, V \ X) = c(S,A) + c(B,D) = 10 + 9 = 19

Note: Edge A → B (cap 2) crosses backward from {A,C,D,T} into {S,B}, so it contributes 0 to the forward cut capacity.

Complexity Analysis: O(E · |f*|)

  • Per-Push Cost: O(V + E) = O(E) using DFS in residual graph G_f.
  • Iteration Bound: Integer capacities ensure Δ ≥ 1 per push ⇒ at most |f*| steps (6 routes here, with 2 reverse-edge cancellations).
  • Vulnerability: Adversarial paths take Θ(|f*|) steps on large integers or fail to terminate on irrational capacities.

Step 1 of 6 Initial State

Shortest-First BFS Trace (4 Routes, 5 BFS Runs)

Strongly Polynomial Proof Outline: O(V · E²)

  • Distance Monotonicity: Residual distances δ(S, v) never decrease across BFS augmentations.
  • Critical Edge Bound: Between consecutive saturations of edge (u, v), reverse edge (v, u) must be used, increasing δ(S, T) by ≥ 2 ⇒ at most V/2 saturations per edge.
  • Total Time: O(V · E) augmentations × O(E) per BFS = O(V · E²).
Notice that all 3-hop paths (Aug 1–3) execute before the 4-hop path S→B→D→C→T (Aug 4), avoiding A→B entirely. Slide 4 of 10

Complexity Analysis: O(V² · E)

  • Level Graph L_G: Keeps only forward edges where level(v) = level(u) + 1 (faded edges are excluded).
  • At Most V − 1 Phases: Each blocking flow strictly increases sink level δ(S, T) (Level 3 → Level 4 → ∞).
  • Blocking Flow Cost: Dead-end pruning finds all paths in a phase in O(V · E) ⇒ O(V² · E) total.

Local Invariants & Excess Return Mechanics

  • Initialization: Set h(S) = |V| = 6 and saturate S→A (10) & S→B (10), creating initial excess e(A)=10, e(B)=10.
  • Push(u, v): Requires e(u) > 0, c_f(u,v) > 0, and exact one-step drop h(u) = h(v) + 1.

Algorithmic Design & Practical Engineering

How Each Paradigm Improves & Practical Selection Guide

Three conceptual leaps from 1956 to 1988 and interactive workload recommendations

Systems Engineering

Three Conceptual Breakthroughs

1. Ford–Fulkerson → Edmonds–Karp:
Fixing path selection to "shortest hop count" bounds pushes at O(V·E) regardless of capacity values: O(E·|f*|) → O(V·E²).
2. Edmonds–Karp → Dinic:
Batching all routes of equal length into one BFS level graph amortizes path search across a blocking flow: O(V·E²) → O(V²·E).
3. Dinic → Push–Relabel:
Replacing global routes with local excess discharge and height labels eliminates level-graph rebuilds: O(V²·E) → O(V³).

Contemporary Research Frontiers (2026)

Recent Breakthroughs in Maximum Flow (2026)

Eliminating continuous interior-point methods in almost-linear time and solving dynamic time-varying flows

2026 Literature

1. Almost-Linear Max-Flow Without Outer IPM Li & Wice (2026)

Source: Maximum Flow Without the Outer IPM (arXiv:2608.17384)

  • Context (2022–2025): Prior E^{1+o(1)} algorithms wrapped graph data structures inside an outer continuous Interior-Point Method (IPM), requiring barrier potentials and Hessian tracking.
  • 2026 Breakthrough: Li and Wice achieve exact E^{1+o(1)} almost-linear time directly through iterative multiplicative-weight updates and dynamic approximate shortest-path / cycle-canceling maintenance.
  • Significance: Bridges the theoretical gap between continuous E^{1+o(1)} complexity and transparent combinatorial flow algorithms.
Runtime: m^{1+o(1)} (Purely Combinatorial / No Outer IPM)

2. Dynamic Maximum Flow Over Time Chawla & Sheridan (2026)

Source: A Special Case of Maximum Flow Over Time with Network Changes (SAND 2026)

  • Problem Setting: Extends Ford & Fulkerson's (1962) Maximum Flow Over Time (MFOT) from static transit networks to dynamic topologies where edge capacities and link availability change over horizon T.
  • 2026 Contribution: Establishes structural optimality conditions and polynomial-time update schemes without rebuilding the entire time-expanded network from scratch.
  • Applications: Real-time packet routing, resilient logistics, and adaptive smart-grid dispatch.
